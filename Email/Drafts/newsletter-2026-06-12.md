We had a friend over for a backyard cook last fall, and before we could stop her, she grabbed the rolling pin off the prep table and ran it straight over a ball of dough that had been resting all day. Thirty seconds later it was flat, even, and completely dead. We've been thinking about that moment again this week while working on our own shaping.

Here's the thing: when dough ferments, it builds up gas bubbles inside the gluten — and those bubbles are the whole reason a crust ends up with that open, chewy texture and a charred cornicione. A rolling pin pops every single one of them and presses the gluten tight and flat, so the crust bakes more like a cracker than a pizza. Hand stretching works with the dough instead of against it — press from the center out, rotate, let gravity do some of the work. You'll tear dough and end up with lopsided ovals at first, and that's normal. If the dough keeps snapping back and fighting you, let it rest ten minutes and try again — patience fixes it, not more force.
