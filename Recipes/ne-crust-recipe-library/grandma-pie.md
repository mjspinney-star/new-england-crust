ingredients:
  - "500 g bread flour"
  - "330 g warm water (66% hydration)"
  - "10 g fine sea salt"
  - "6 g instant yeast"
  - "20 g olive oil, plus 3 tbsp for the pan"
  - "2 cloves garlic, grated into the pan oil"
  - "300 g low-moisture mozzarella, shredded"
  - "1 can (28 oz) whole tomatoes, hand-crushed with 1 tsp salt, 1 tsp oregano, 1 tbsp olive oil (uncooked)"
  - "Fresh basil and grated pecorino to finish"
instructions:
  - step: "Quick dough"
    text: "Mix and knead 6 minutes, rise 1 hour. Grandma pie's whole identity is impatience — it was invented by grandmothers who weren't waiting around for a long proof."
  - step: "Garlic-oil the pan"
    text: "Stir the grated garlic into the pan oil and coat a half-sheet pan. Stretch the dough to the corners, resting once if needed. Proof only 30 minutes — this is a THIN pie, not a Sicilian."
  - step: "Cheese first, then sauce"
    text: "Mozzarella straight on the dough, edge to edge, then the crushed tomato sauce in dollops and stripes over the cheese. Inverting the layers keeps the thin crust from going soggy."
  - step: "Bake"
    text: "475°F on the lowest rack, 16–18 minutes, until the underside is golden-fried from the garlic oil and the sauce has concentrated."
  - step: "Finish and square it up"
    text: "Basil, pecorino, one more thread of oil. Cut into little squares — grandma pie is meant to disappear three squares at a time."

The grandma pie is the sheet-pan pizza for people who decided on pizza two hours ago, not two days ago. Thin, garlicky, cheese hiding under the sauce — and the single best recipe in this library for feeding a soccer team on short notice. Ask me how I know.
