We used to grab whatever hard cheese was closest to the microplane. That was a mistake more than once — sharp Pecorino dumped on a delicate Margherita, or mild Parmigiano wasted on a pie that needed real bite.

Here's the actual difference: Pecorino Romano is sheep's-milk, sharp, salty, aggressive — it cuts through fat and richness fast, so it earns its spot on sausage, guanciale, or a heavy olive oil base. Parmigiano-Reggiano is cow's-milk, nuttier, softer — it backs up a tomato-forward pie without fighting the sauce, and it's the better call on delicate toppings like fresh mushrooms or prosciutto.

And sometimes neither belongs. A clean Margherita, or an already-salty pie like a clam pie or a potato-rosemary white, doesn't need a hard grating cheese piled on top.

Rule we go by: sharpness and richness, Pecorino. Tomato and delicacy, Parmigiano. Already salty, set both down.
