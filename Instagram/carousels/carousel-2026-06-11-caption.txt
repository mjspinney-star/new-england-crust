Stop reaching for the rolling pin. It's quietly killing your crust.

Every hour your dough ferments, it builds up gas bubbles in the gluten — and that's the whole reason a good crust ends up open, chewy, and charred at the rim. A rolling pin pops every single one of those bubbles, flattening the dough into something that bakes more like a cracker than a pizza. Hand stretching works with the dough instead: press from center to edge, rotate, and let gravity do the work so the rim stays thick and the gas stays put. It takes a few tries (you will tear some dough), but the difference is obvious the second it hits a hot oven.
