Most people grab whatever yeast is on the shelf and hope for the best. That works — until a slow, sluggish dough in November or an overproofed mess in July traces straight back to it, not the recipe.

Instant yeast is our default — it goes straight into dry flour, no proofing, no dissolving, and it ferments reliably whether we're doing a same-day dough or a 72-hour cold ferment. Active dry yeast still works, but it asks more of you: dissolve it in 100–110°F water, wait ten minutes, and hope your kitchen didn't cool the water first. Fresh yeast is genuinely worth trying once — noticeably good flavor in long ferments — but it only keeps about two weeks, so we don't build our routine around it.
