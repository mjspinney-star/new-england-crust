Every pizza oven ad shows the same bubbly, leopard-spotted crust. What they don't show: your first three pies underdone on the bottom, burnt on top, and a stone that wasn't actually up to temperature. We've been there — and there's one spec that would have saved us.

True Neapolitan pizza wants 800–900°F for a 60 to 90 second cook. Below that, you're baking, not firing. Here's where five ovens under $500 land: Ooni Koda 12 and Ooni Fyra both reach about 950°F in 60 to 90 seconds — the Fyra just takes more tending since you're feeding pellets, not turning a knob. Solo Stove Pi tops out around 900°F but runs a longer 90-second to 2.5-minute cook, which buys you more time to rotate and recover from a rough launch. Cuisinart's 3-in-1 also gets to about 900°F, for $100 to $150 less than the others, though the build is lighter and needs more attention. The Ninja Woodfire is the outlier — it tops out around 700°F and takes 4 to 7 minutes, so you won't get a true Neapolitan char, but it also smokes, roasts, and air-fries, which none of the others do.

Our pick for most people: Ooni Koda 12. If you want one appliance that does everything: Ninja Woodfire. Either way, an infrared thermometer is the $20 tool that tells you what your stone is actually doing — the display only knows air temp.
