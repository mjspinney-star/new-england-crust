## The Pizza Nobody Asks For (And Then Eats)

Anchovy pizza has a reputation. Say the word and someone makes a face. But built right, with the salt balanced by mild olives and a restrained hand on the sauce, it's the pizza doubters quietly go back for.

This is not a subtle pizza. But it is a balanced one.

## What You Need

This comes together fast. The work is mostly in how you build it.

**For one 12-inch pizza:**

- Your prepared dough ball, room temperature
- 3 tablespoons crushed canned tomatoes — just tomatoes, no added seasoning
- 8 to 10 oil-packed anchovy fillets
- A small handful of **Castelvetrano olives**, pitted and torn in half
- Low-moisture mozzarella, torn — not shredded
- A drizzle of good olive oil after the bake
- Optional: a few dried chili flakes before it goes in

We use Castelvetrano specifically because they are buttery and mild. They pull the anchovies back from the edge. Kalamata works too, but the brine is sharper — adjust to your taste.

## How We Build It

Stretch the dough and lay it on a well-semolina'd peel. On the Ninja, brush it with oil and par-bake it for about 1 minute 30 seconds first (more on that below), then build on the set crust. Spread the crushed tomatoes thin — this is not a saucy pizza. You want just enough to give the cheese something to hold onto.

Tear the mozzarella across the surface. Do not pile it. Then lay the anchovy fillets evenly over the cheese. They will melt into the bake. Scatter the olives. Add chili flakes if you want heat.

That is it. Do not overthink the assembly.

## Cooking It Outside

We cook this the way we cook every pizza on our Ninja Woodfire: preheat to 550°F (about 20 minutes, with the stone reading around 540°F on an infrared thermometer), brush the stretched dough with oil and par-bake it for about 1 minute 30 seconds, then top it and bake for about 5 minutes without turning it. The anchovies soften into the cheese and the edges pick up color. A little pellet smoke plays well with the brininess.

If you're cooking in a hotter, Neapolitan-style oven (850°F and up), the whole bake is closer to 90 seconds with a turn halfway, and the anchovy edges crisp more.

Pull it when the crust is golden and the cheese has some color.

## The Finish

Right out of the oven, hit it with a thin drizzle of olive oil. Nothing else. No fresh herbs — they compete here. Let the anchovy and olive do what they came to do.

Cut it and put it out before you do anything else. It eats better hot.

## Honest Assessment

The anchovy smell while it cooks is strong. With guests around, some people will hover and some will take a step back. Do not pre-explain it. Just let them try a piece.

The salt level is high. That is not a flaw, but it does mean this pizza wants a cold drink next to it.

**Make it once for a skeptic and see what happens.**
