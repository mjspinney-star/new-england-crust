## The Pizza Nobody Asks For (And Then Eats)

Every time we put this pizza out, someone makes a face. "Anchovies?" And then that same person goes back for a second slice before anyone else does. We have seen this happen enough times that it stopped surprising us.

This is not a subtle pizza. But it is a balanced one.

## What You Need

This comes together fast. The work is mostly in how you build it.

**For one 12-inch pizza:**

- Your prepared dough ball, room temperature
- 3 tablespoons crushed canned tomatoes — just tomatoes, no added seasoning
- 8 to 10 oil-packed anchovy fillets
- A small handful of **Castelvetrano olives**, pitted and torn in half
- Low-moisture mozzarella, torn — not shredded
- A drizzle of good olive oil after the bake
- Optional: a few dried chili flakes before it goes in

We use Castelvetrano specifically because they are buttery and mild. They pull the anchovies back from the edge. Kalamata works too, but the brine is sharper — adjust to your taste.

## How We Build It

Stretch the dough and lay it on a well-semolina'd peel. Spread the crushed tomatoes thin — this is not a saucy pizza. You want just enough to give the cheese something to hold onto.

Tear the mozzarella across the surface. Do not pile it. Then lay the anchovy fillets evenly over the cheese. They will melt into the bake. Scatter the olives. Add chili flakes if you want heat.

That is it. Do not overthink the assembly.

## Cooking It Outside

We have made this on the Ooni Koda 12 more times than we can count. Stone around 850°F, maybe 90 seconds, one turn at the halfway mark. The anchovies go slightly crispy at the edges where they catch the heat — that part is not a mistake, it is the point.

If you are running the Ninja Woodfire, you will cook longer at a lower ceiling temperature. The anchovy character softens a bit more. Still good. The pellet smoke actually plays well with the brininess.

Pull it the moment the crust is spotted and the cheese has some color. Do not wait for fully golden — this pizza wants a little char.

## The Finish

Right out of the oven, hit it with a thin drizzle of olive oil. Nothing else. No fresh herbs — they compete here. Let the anchovy and olive do what they came to do.

Cut it and put it out before you do anything else. It eats better hot.

## Honest Assessment

The anchovy smell while it cooks is strong. If you are doing a backyard session with guests around — a September evening, hoodie weather, fire going — some people will hover. Some will take a step back. Do not pre-explain it. Just let them try a piece.

The salt level is high. That is not a flaw, but it does mean this pizza wants a cold drink next to it.

**Every skeptic we have cooked this for has asked what was on it after the fact. Make it once and you will understand why.**
