Here is the situation we have all been in. You are two hours into a backyard pizza session, the stone is ripping hot, and someone asks if they should tear some basil on before the launch or just grab the jar from the spice cabinet.

It is not a small question. The answer depends entirely on how your oven cooks.

## What High Heat Does to Basil

Fresh basil burns fast. At 900°F to 950°F — the kind of temps you are running in an Ooni Koda 12 — fresh leaves on top of a pie before the launch will turn black and bitter in under a minute. Not charred in a good way. Just scorched.

Dried basil has the opposite problem. It can handle the heat, but it loses whatever fragrance it had and contributes almost nothing to the finished pie.

Neither one wins if you use it the wrong way.

## The Timing Fix for Fresh Basil

Fresh basil belongs after the bake. Pull the pie, let it rest for thirty seconds, then tear the leaves on top. The residual heat wilts them just enough — you get the aroma, the brightness, and the color without the burn.

This is the approach used on a proper margherita, and it works just as well on a backyard patio in June as it does anywhere else. You will learn this after one or two cooks where you forget to wait.

## When Dried Basil Actually Earns Its Spot

There is one honest use case. Dried basil mixed directly into a sauce — not sprinkled on top, but stirred in and given time to bloom — can add a low, earthy background note that works on longer-cooked pies. Think sheet pan nights or anything going into a home oven at lower temps.

For outdoor high-heat cooking, though, it rarely pulls its weight on its own.

## The Honest Catch with Fresh Basil in New England

Fresh basil is a summer-only ingredient here. In June, July, and August you can grab a plant at any farm stand for a couple of dollars and keep it on the back steps. Come September the nights get cold, the plant sulks, and by October it is done.

Dried basil is there all year. We keep a jar around because not every pizza night lines up with peak garden season — and that is fine. Just use it where it actually works.

## Real-World Verdict

Fresh basil added after the bake is the right call for outdoor high-heat pizza, full stop. Dried basil has a narrower use case and it is not on top of a 950°F pie.

---

**Try this next:** At your next session, tear four or five leaves of fresh basil onto the finished pie before you slice it. Give it thirty seconds. That small timing shift is the difference between a garnish and a flat, burnt afterthought.
