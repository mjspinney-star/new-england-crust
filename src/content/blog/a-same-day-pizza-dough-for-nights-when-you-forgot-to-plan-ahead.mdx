It is 4 PM on a Saturday and someone just asked what's for dinner. You have flour, yeast, olive oil, and nothing in the fridge that qualifies as a plan. We have been here more times than we would like to admit.

The good news: you can have workable dough ready in about two to three hours. It will not taste like a 72-hour cold ferment. But it will make real pizza — crispy bottom, good chew, holds toppings without getting soggy. That is enough for a weeknight.

## What You Need

- 500g **bread flour** (or 00 flour if you have it)
- 325g warm water — around 95°F, not hot
- 7g **instant yeast** — not active dry, not rapid rise
- 10g kosher salt
- 15g olive oil

Instant yeast is the move here. It dissolves directly into the dough without proofing, and it gets going fast. We keep a jar of it in the fridge specifically for days like this.

## The Mix

Combine the flour and yeast first. Add the water and olive oil, mix until shaggy, then add the salt. We add salt after the initial mix — not because it will ruin anything if you don't, but because it buys the yeast a few extra minutes before hitting anything inhibiting.

Knead for 8 to 10 minutes by hand, or 5 to 6 minutes in a stand mixer on medium. You want a smooth, slightly tacky ball — not sticky, not dry.

## The Rise

This is where same-day dough lives or dies. You need warmth.

Set the oven to the lowest setting — usually around 170°F — then turn it off and crack the door for two minutes. Put your covered dough bowl in there. That residual heat creates a proofing environment that cuts your rise time significantly. Aim for 60 to 90 minutes, until the dough has roughly doubled.

In August, a warm New England kitchen actually helps here. A 75°F room works fine too — just give it closer to 90 minutes.

## Ball It Out

Once the bulk rise is done, divide and **ball** the dough into two or three portions depending on your oven size. Let them rest covered on the counter for 30 to 45 minutes. Do not skip this. That second rest is what makes the dough extensible enough to stretch without snapping back.

**Verdict: The rest after balling matters more than most people expect.** Rush it and the dough will fight you at the peel.

## Honest Assessment

Same-day dough is less complex in flavor. The crust will be good — not great. You will notice the difference if you have made the 72-hour version. Anyone eating pizza on a Saturday night probably will not.

The texture is also a little more bready than we prefer, especially if your bake is on the longer side. Aim for a hot, fast cook if you can — 800°F and above, Ooni Koda 12 territory. High heat compensates for what fermentation time did not build.

## Try This Next

Make a double batch, use one tonight, and put the second set of dough balls in the fridge. By tomorrow night you have a 24-hour cold-fermented dough with zero extra effort.
