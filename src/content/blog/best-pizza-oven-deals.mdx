## How pizza oven deals actually work

Retailers discount across categories during the big events, but the deals aren't uniform. Big-ticket outdoor cooking gear tends to see meaningful discounts — 15–25% on ovens in the $300–$500 range isn't unusual. Accessories in the $20–$50 range often drop too, sometimes more aggressively than the hardware.

What doesn't move much: highly niche items with small inventory, and anything that's already been discounted recently. Pricing algorithms are aware of their own history.

The other thing worth knowing: you don't need to find a special "deals page" at the retailer. If you're watching a product and the price drops during an event, that's the deal. The links below point directly to the product pages — check them during any event and you'll see whatever's running.

## The accessories that are actually worth buying on sale

Accessories are where a sale event can quietly save you more money than the hardware. A few worth having on your radar:

**<AffiliateLink id="ninja-peel" label="Metal turning peel" />** — Not optional. You need one to rotate your pizza inside the oven. The good ones run $30–$40. If you see a well-reviewed perforated aluminum peel drop below $25, that's an easy purchase.

**<AffiliateLink id="etekcity-scale" label="Digital kitchen scale" />** — Dough by weight is more consistent than dough by volume. Every time. A decent scale runs $10–$20 normally, often less during an event.

**<AffiliateLink id="chef-pomodoro-proofing-box" label="Dough proofing container" />** — Not strictly necessary, but genuinely more convenient than a bowl and plastic wrap. Measurement markings, a lid that actually seals, stackable. The kind of thing that costs $15–$20 and you wonder why you waited.

**<AffiliateLink id="infrared-thermometer" label="Infrared thermometer" />** — Already mentioned in my oven comparison, but worth repeating here: this $20 tool tells you your actual stone temperature, not the air temperature your oven's display shows. The gap between those two numbers is where bad pizza comes from. Buy one now if you don't have one.

## What I'd skip

Branded "pizza night" bundles — the ones that come with a branded peel, a cutter, and a serving board with your oven's logo on it. They're marked up to sell. You can buy better individual versions of each item for less.

Anything marketed as a "pizza oven cover set" with multiple sizes — find the specific cover made for your oven model. Generic covers that "fit most ovens" usually don't fit any oven well.

## The sale calendar

These are the windows I actually watch, in order through the year:

### Prime Day (mid-July)
Amazon's mid-summer event, usually four days in July. Historically the best window for the Ninja Woodfire and for accessories. The best prices tend to appear in the first 12–18 hours and again in the final stretch; the middle period sometimes sees restocks and lightning deals on accessories.

### October Prime event (early–mid October)
Amazon's fall event — smaller than July, but outdoor cooking gear often gets end-of-season pricing on top of event pricing. If you can cook through a New England fall (you can — that's half the fun), this is a sleeper window.

### Black Friday & Cyber Monday (late November)
The deepest oven discounts of the year, and the one window where the brands themselves — Ooni and Solo Stove especially — run aggressive sales on their own sites alongside the retailers. If you're gifting an oven for the holidays, buy it here, not in December.

### Everything else
One-off flash sales happen year-round, especially on accessories. That's what the price signals above are for: if a listed price beats the signal, it's a deal regardless of what the calendar says.
