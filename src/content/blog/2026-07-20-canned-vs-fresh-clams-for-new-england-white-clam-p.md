We have made a lot of white clam pizzas. Some of them were great. A few were genuinely bad — rubbery, fishy, or so salty the whole pie was a write-off. In almost every case, the clams were the reason.

So here is where we actually land after cooking both options back to back.

## The Case for Canned Clams

Canned clams — specifically **chopped canned clams** in clam juice — are consistent. You know what you are getting. They are already cooked, they are already cut, and the juice in the can is legitimately useful for finishing a garlic oil base.

The texture will not blow anyone away. But on a pizza that spends 90 seconds in a 900-degree oven, fresh clams can overcook just as fast. Canned clams, already cooked, are actually a little more forgiving in that environment.

We use **Bumble Bee or Bar Harbor chopped clams** when we are cooking mid-week or when we have six people showing up and do not want to deal with shucking. Drain them, pat them lightly, hit the pie with a little of the reserved juice in the oil — done.

Canned clams are the practical choice, and there is nothing wrong with that.

## The Case for Fresh Clams

Fresh clams are better. We will just say that plainly.

Littlenecks — shucked and left in their own liquor — have a cleaner brine and a texture that actually holds up if you time the cook right. Living in New England means we are not exactly driving far to find them. Summer fish markets from the Cape up through Maine keep them in stock, and they are cheap by the pound when local boats are running.

The work is real, though. Shucking a dozen littlenecks for one pizza is not a big deal. Shucking for four pizzas on a Saturday with friends over is a different conversation. Your hands will smell like the ocean until Tuesday.

The other honest note: fresh clams on a very hot oven — an Ooni Koda 12 *[Affiliate link: Ooni Koda 12 — Ooni]* running at 950°F — need to go on late or they tighten up fast. We add them in the last 20 seconds of the cook, or pull the pie just before they fully set.

Fresh clams reward the extra effort when you have the time and access.

## What We Actually Do

Weeknight, solo cook, or short outdoor window before a nor'easter rolls in — canned, no debate.

Weekend cook with time, good company, or a trip to the fish market already on the calendar — fresh littlenecks, shucked that morning, every time.

The rest of the pie matters too. A strong garlic-olive oil base, good low-moisture mozzarella used lightly, and a hit of **Pecorino Romano** after the bake will do more for this pizza than the clam debate will.

## Try This Next

If you are making white clam pizza for the first time, start with canned. Get the build right — dough, oil, cheese ratio, cook time. Once that feels dialed in, swap in fresh clams and you will immediately taste the difference. That comparison, made back to back, teaches you more than reading about it will.
