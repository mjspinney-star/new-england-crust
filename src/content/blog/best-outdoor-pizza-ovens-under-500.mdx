## The one number that matters: heat

A true Neapolitan pizza cooks at roughly 800–900°F in 60 to 90 seconds. At that heat the crust puffs almost instantly, the edge picks up leopard-spot char, and the middle stays soft. A kitchen oven tops out around 500°F, which bakes pizza rather than firing it.

Here's the honest version for this price range: three of these five ovens can hit Neapolitan heat. Two top out at 700°F — including ours. That one fact sorts most buyers faster than anything else in this guide.

Whatever you buy, get an <AffiliateLink id="infrared-thermometer" label="infrared thermometer" />. The oven's display reads air temperature. The stone is what cooks the bottom of your pizza, and the two don't always agree. We check the stone before every launch.

## The one we own: Ninja Woodfire OO101

### Why we bought it

We looked hard at every oven on this list. What tipped it was ease. The Ninja plugs into a wall. There's no propane tank to fill, no fire to tend, and a temperature dial that does what it says. We wanted pizza night to be something we'd actually do on a Tuesday, and electric was the lowest-friction way to get there.

### How we actually cook pizza on it

This is our routine after more than a year:

1. **Preheat to 550°F.** It takes about 20 minutes to get there.
2. **Check the stone.** Our infrared thermometer usually reads about 540°F when it's ready.
3. **Par-bake the crust.** We brush the stretched dough with oil and give it about 1 minute 30 seconds on its own before any toppings go on. This sets the base so it doesn't go soft under the sauce.
4. **Top it and cook for about 5 minutes.**
5. **Leave it alone.** No rotating, no flipping. We put it in, close the door, and wait.

Yes, the oven goes to 700°F. We cook at 550 because that's what gives us the crust we like with this routine.

![A cheese and tomato pizza cooking on the seasoned square pizza stone inside the Ninja Woodfire, door open](/assets/blog/best-outdoor-pizza-ovens-under-500/ninja-woodfire-pizza-on-stone.jpg)

That stone started out pale beige. The dark patina is a year of pizza, and it's supposed to look like that.

### What comes out

A golden, crisp crust with some browning and blistering on the edge — not leopard-spotted Neapolitan char. If you've seen photos of 60-second pizza from a 900°F oven, this is a different animal: sturdier, more even, and very good. ### Beyond pizza

This is where the Ninja earns its spot. It's an 8-in-1 — Pizza, Max Roast, Specialty Roast, Broil, Bake, Smoke, Dehydrate, and Keep Warm — and a half cup of pellets adds real wood smoke at any temperature. We've done pulled pork and smoked chicken on it, and both came out genuinely tender. A dedicated pizza oven can't do that.

### A year in: what we'd tell a friend

Honestly, we haven't had a real problem with it. The limits are the ones Ninja tells you about up front:

- **700°F is the ceiling.** If a true 60-second Neapolitan is what you're after, this isn't the oven.
- **You need an outlet.** The cord is short, so plan on a 12-gauge <AffiliateLink id="extension-cord" label="outdoor-rated extension cord" /> unless your outlet is right where you cook.
- **It lives on the patio.** Ours sits on a <AffiliateLink id="keter-stand" label="Keter Unity cart" /> with the peel hanging off the side. It's not something you'll toss in the car for the beach.
- **Cover it.** It's weather-resistant, not New England–proof. We keep a <AffiliateLink id="ninja-cover" label="cover" /> on it between cooks.

**The verdict:** The best fit in this price range for anyone who wants pizza *and* a smoker and roaster, with the least amount of fuss.

## The other four: what we found while shopping

We didn't buy these, so there's nothing below about how they behave on a Saturday night. This is the research: what each one is, what the specs say, and who it's built for.

### Ooni Koda 2 — if Neapolitan heat is the goal

The Koda 2 is Ooni's current compact gas oven: propane, a 14-inch stone, rated to 950°F, about 35 pounds, $499. That puts it right at the top of this budget, and it's the most direct route here to fast, high-heat pizza.

A note if you've seen older guides, including the earlier version of this one: the 12-inch **Koda 12** is Ooni's first-generation model. As of September 2026 it sits on Ooni's last-chance page at $299 and has been out of stock at major retailers for weeks at a time. If you find one, it's a lighter, cheaper way into the same idea. Just don't count on it.

**The trade-off vs. ours:** far more heat and speed, no smoking or roasting, and a propane tank to manage.

### Ooni Fyra 12 — wood pellets, real fire

The Fyra 12 burns hardwood pellets from a gravity-fed hopper. Ooni rates it at 950°F in about 15 minutes and roughly 60-second pizzas, and it weighs about 22 pounds. It runs around $349 at retailers.

The appeal is flavor from an actual live fire. The cost is that a pellet fire is something you manage, and wind and weather matter more than they do with gas or electric.

**The trade-off vs. ours:** genuine wood-fired heat, more hands-on cooking, and it's a pizza oven first and last.

### Solo Stove Pi Prime — the gas option from Solo Stove

The Pi Prime is Solo Stove's propane-only oven (the original Pi burns wood, with gas as an add-on). Solo Stove rates it above 950°F, preheating in about 15 minutes and cooking pizzas in as little as 90 seconds. It weighs a bit over 30 pounds, fits pizzas up to about 13 inches, and includes a cover. List price is $399.99, and it goes on sale often.

**The trade-off vs. ours:** Neapolitan-level heat without fire-tending, for about what the Ninja lists at — but it only does pizza.

### Cuisinart 3-in-1 Pizza Oven Plus — the budget multitasker

This one gets misdescribed a lot, and our earlier version of this guide got it wrong too. Per Cuisinart, the 3-in-1 runs on propane, reaches 700°F (not 900°F), and cooks pizza in 6 to 8 minutes. It uses a 13-inch cordierite stone and swaps between pizza stone, griddle, and grill grate. It's also heavier than it looks, at around 56 pounds. List price is $249.99.

**The trade-off vs. ours:** a similar temperature ceiling, a griddle and grill instead of a smoker, propane instead of an outlet, and the lowest price here.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can the Ninja Woodfire make Neapolitan pizza?**
Not true Neapolitan. It tops out at 700°F, and that style wants 800–900°F. What it makes is a crisp, golden, evenly cooked pizza that we're happy to serve to anyone.

**What temperature do you cook pizza at on the Ninja Woodfire?**
We preheat to 550°F for about 20 minutes, confirm the stone is around 540°F with an infrared thermometer, par-bake the oiled crust for about 1 minute 30 seconds, then top it and cook for about 5 minutes without touching it.

**Do I need a propane tank?**
For the Ooni Koda 2, Solo Stove Pi Prime, and Cuisinart 3-in-1, yes. The Ooni Fyra 12 runs on wood pellets. The Ninja Woodfire runs on electricity and only uses pellets for smoke flavor.

**Can I use one of these on a wooden deck?**
Ours sits on a cart on our deck. Whatever you buy, follow the manufacturer's clearance and surface guidance — open-flame ovens especially. When in doubt, use a heat-resistant mat or cook on stone or concrete.

**What size peel do I need?**
Match the peel to the oven's stone. For the Ninja, the <AffiliateLink id="ninja-peel" label="Ninja perforated peel" /> is sized for the opening and it's the one we use every time.

**Can I leave an outdoor pizza oven outside all year?**
Most are weather-resistant, not weatherproof. In New England we'd cover any oven that lives outside, and follow the manufacturer's winter storage guidance for anything electric.
