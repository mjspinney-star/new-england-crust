## The Situation in Late September

There is a particular kind of afternoon in late September — hoodie weather, the smell of leaves, the oven still worth firing up before it gets dark at 6 p.m. — where this pizza makes complete sense.

We were skeptical the first time someone suggested gorgonzola and apple together on a pizza. It sounds like a charcuterie board fell on a dough round. But it works, and it works well enough that we have made it nearly every October since.

## What You Are Working With

This is a white pizza — no tomato sauce. The base is a thin layer of olive oil, a little garlic, and some fresh thyme. Keep it restrained. The toppings are loud enough.

**The apple:** Use a Honeycrisp or a Granny Smith. Honeycrisp is sweeter and holds up better under heat. Granny Smith stays tarter, which cuts through the cheese more aggressively. We prefer Honeycrisp, but either works. Slice thin — about an eighth of an inch — and pat dry.

**The cheese:** **Gorgonzola dolce** is what you want here, not the aged stuff. Dolce is creamier and milder. Crumble it loosely. It will melt into pockets, which is exactly what should happen.

**The walnuts:** Toast them in a dry pan for three or four minutes before they go on the pizza. Untoasted walnuts taste like nothing. Toasted walnuts taste like fall.

**Optional additions:** A drizzle of honey after the bake — not before — adds something. A handful of arugula tossed on after the pull works if you want a little bitterness to balance everything out.

## How to Build and Cook It

Stretch your dough to about 10 to 12 inches. Brush with olive oil, add the garlic and thyme, then lay down the apple slices in a single layer. Crumble the gorgonzola over that. Scatter the walnuts last.

At high heat — 850 to 950°F in a Neapolitan-style oven — this cooks in 60 to 90 seconds. Watch the cheese and the walnuts closely. Gorgonzola bubbles fast and the walnuts can catch.

If you are running at lower temps on something like a Ninja Woodfire, expect four to six minutes. The apple will soften more, which is not a problem.

## Honest Assessment

The cheese can overpower everything if you go heavy. Less gorgonzola than you think. The apple also releases moisture, so dry your slices before they go on — a damp pizza is a sad pizza.

This is not a crowd-pleaser for every crowd. If you are cooking for people who treat pizza as a vehicle for pepperoni, save this for another night. But if your group is open to it, it tends to surprise people.

The balance of tart, creamy, and bitter-crunchy is the whole point of this pizza.

## Try This Next

Make this on the first cold weekend in October. Fire the oven, pour something warm, and see what your backyard patio can still do before winter shuts it down for five months.
