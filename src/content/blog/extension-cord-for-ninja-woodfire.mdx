The Ninja Woodfire draws a serious amount of current — enough that a lightweight indoor extension cord is a real problem, not just a mild inconvenience. If you've ever had an outdoor appliance trip a breaker or run sluggishly, a undersized cord is usually why. Getting this right before you set up is worth the five minutes it takes to understand.

## Why the Gauge Number Matters

Wire gauge is rated by AWG — American Wire Gauge — and the lower the number, the thicker the wire and the more current it can safely carry. For a high-draw appliance like the Ninja Woodfire, most electricians and appliance guides recommend a 12 AWG cord at minimum. A 14 AWG cord is borderline for longer runs. A 16 AWG cord — the kind bundled with cheap indoor appliances — is too thin and can cause voltage drop, which stresses the oven's components and may trip your breaker.

The takeaway: buy a 12 AWG outdoor extension cord. Don't guess at the gauge — it's printed right on the jacket.

## Outdoor Rating Is Not Optional

An outdoor-rated cord has a jacket designed to handle moisture, UV exposure, and temperature swings. In coastal New England, those conditions aren't edge cases — they're the norm from roughly October through May, and summer mornings on the water bring plenty of dew. An indoor cord used outside can degrade faster than you'd expect, and a cracked jacket near a water source is a genuine hazard.

Look for a cord marked "W" in its designation (for example, SJTW or SJOW) — that "W" indicates it's rated for outdoor, damp, or wet locations. If the cord packaging doesn't say outdoor rated, assume it isn't.

The takeaway: the outdoor rating on the jacket isn't marketing language. It's a safety specification.

## How Long a Cord Do You Actually Need?

Shorter is better — every foot of cord adds a small amount of resistance, and resistance means voltage drop under load. Buy only as much length as your setup genuinely requires. Measure the distance from your outlet to where the oven sits, add a few feet of slack so the cord isn't pulled taut, and buy that length. Bundling up 25 feet of extra cord on a 50-foot run wastes money and adds unnecessary resistance.

Our Ninja sits on a Keter Unity cart on the deck. The outlet is close enough that a 25-foot cord is more than sufficient for our setup. Your deck layout will differ, but the principle is the same: measure first, then buy.

The takeaway: a 25-foot 12 AWG outdoor cord covers most deck setups. A 50-foot cord is only worth it if you genuinely need the run.

## Grounded, Three-Prong — No Exceptions

The Ninja Woodfire has a three-prong grounded plug. The extension cord needs a three-prong grounded receptacle. Never use an adapter to defeat the ground pin. That ground is part of the appliance's safety design, and bypassing it is not worth the risk.

## A Note on GFCI Outlets

If your outdoor outlet is GFCI-protected — most are, and rightly so — be aware that some high-draw appliances trip GFCI outlets occasionally, especially on a long cord. If that happens, the fix is usually a shorter cord or a dedicated circuit, not a workaround. Check with a licensed electrician if you're having repeated trips.
