Late August is when fig season and outdoor pizza season overlap in New England, and the window is short. You get maybe three good weekends before the figs are gone and the evenings turn cold enough to need a jacket just to tend the oven. This pizza is worth building those weekends around.

## The Problem Most People Run Into

Sweet and bitter are natural counterweights. But on a pizza, both can go wrong fast. Too much fig jam and the whole thing reads like dessert. Too much arugula piled on before the bake and it wilts into something soggy and sad. The prosciutto can disappear into the background if you are not careful about placement.

The fix is sequencing. What goes on before the bake, what goes on right after, and what gets finished at the table.

## What You Need

- One stretched dough round, roughly 12 inches
- **Fig jam** — two to three tablespoons, spread thin like a sauce base
- Fresh mozzarella, torn — not shredded, not pre-sliced
- Four to five slices of prosciutto di Parma
- A big handful of arugula — dressed, not dry
- Good olive oil and a few grinds of black pepper
- Optional: shaved Parmigiano or a drizzle of balsamic reduction at the end

## Build It in This Order

Spread the fig jam directly on the stretched dough. Keep it sparse — you want coverage, not thickness. Lay down the torn mozzarella. Leave gaps. This is not a cheese-heavy pizza.

Do not put the prosciutto on before the bake. It will dry out and turn papery. Launch the pizza as-is.

Cook it at around 700 to 800°F for 90 seconds to two minutes, rotating once. If you are running an Ooni Koda 12 or Solo Stove Pi at full temperature, watch it — the jam can scorch around the edges at 950°F. A slightly dialed-back flame helps here.

## After the Bake Is Where It Comes Together

Pull the pizza. While it rests for 60 seconds, dress your arugula. A drizzle of olive oil, a small pinch of salt, a squeeze of lemon if you have it. The dressing matters — dry arugula on hot pizza is bitter without balance.

Lay the prosciutto over the hot cheese first. Then pile the dressed arugula on top. The residual heat softens the prosciutto just enough without cooking it. The arugula wilts slightly at the edges where it touches the cheese — that is what you want.

Finish with black pepper and, if you are using it, a thin drizzle of balsamic reduction. Go light. This pizza is already doing a lot.

## Honest Quirks

The fig jam can bubble and stick if your stone is very hot. A little semolina under the dough before launch helps. The arugula shrinks more than you expect — start with more than looks right.

This pizza does not reheat well. Eat it fresh, cut at the table, immediately.

## Try This Next

If you have extra fig jam, spread a thin layer on flatbread, top with crumbled blue cheese, and run it under the broiler for three minutes. It is a ten-minute thing that works well while you are waiting for the oven to come up to temp.
