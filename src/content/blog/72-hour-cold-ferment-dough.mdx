Mix dough at noon, stretch it at six, and you'll find out at seven why same-day pizza tastes like warm flour and fights you all the way onto the peel.

The fix isn't a fancier technique. It's time. Mix the dough a day or two ahead, let it sit in the fridge, and almost everything downstream gets easier.

If you just want the recipe, it's here: **[our Caputo 00 breadmaker dough](/recipes/caputo-breadmaker-dough/)**, with exact weights and steps. This post is the why.

## What a cold ferment actually does

Slowing fermentation down in the fridge does three things same-day dough can't:

1. **Flavor.** The yeast works slowly and enzymes break down starches, so the same handful of ingredients tastes deeper and a little sweeter.
2. **Extensibility.** The gluten relaxes, so the dough opens without snapping back. That matters most when you're stretching on the deck and the oven is already hot.
3. **Schedule.** The dough waits for you. Mix it a day or two before pizza night and there's nothing to rush when people show up.

## The dough we use

Our everyday dough is short and simple, and it's mixed in a bread machine:

- <AffiliateLink id="caputo-flour" label="Caputo Pizzeria 00 flour" /> — 500 g
- Cool water — 325 g (65% hydration)
- <AffiliateLink id="baleine-sea-salt" label="Baleine fine sea salt" /> — 13 g
- <AffiliateLink id="caputo-yeast" label="Caputo Lievito dry yeast" /> — 1.5 g
- Olive oil — optional

The <AffiliateLink id="cuisinart-bread-maker" label="Cuisinart CBK-110" /> handles the mixing and first rise on its Dough cycle. When it finishes, we divide the dough into balls (250–300 g each), set them in a floured proofing tray, cover it, and put it in the fridge. The [full recipe](/recipes/caputo-breadmaker-dough/) has every step.

![Six balls of Caputo 00 pizza dough in a floured proofing tray, ready for the fridge](/assets/blog/72-hour-cold-ferment-dough/dough-balls-in-proofing-tray.jpg)

The small amount of yeast is deliberate. With 1.5 g per 500 g of flour, the dough rises slowly enough to spend a day or two in the fridge without overproofing.

Weigh everything. Baker's percentages only work by weight, and a <AffiliateLink id="etekcity-scale" label="digital kitchen scale" /> is the one tool we'd call non-negotiable.

## How long to ferment

- **24 hours:** a real improvement over same-day dough.
- **48 hours:** where the flavor really comes through.
- **Longer:** you can push it to 72 hours and the dough still handles well, but that's not what we do. We go 24–48 hours; past about 48 you're mostly buying convenience, not a lot more flavor.

Whatever you choose, pull the dough out of the fridge 1–2 hours before you bake. Cold dough tears; room-temperature dough stretches. If you're not sure it's ready, [here's how to tell](/blog/2026-06-08-how-to-tell-when-your-dough-is-actually-ready-to-o/).

## How it bakes on our Ninja Woodfire

We preheat the Ninja to 550°F, which takes about 20 minutes, and check the stone with an infrared thermometer. It usually reads around 540°F. We brush the stretched dough with oil and par-bake it for about 1 minute 30 seconds, then top it and bake for about 5 minutes without turning it.

The result is an evenly golden crust with some blistering on the edge. It won't leopard like a 60-second Neapolitan from a 900°F oven, and it doesn't need to. The cold-fermented dough is what gives it flavor and a chew that same-day dough never has.

## One tip for humid days

If the dough feels sticky and hard to handle on a humid day, hold back 2–3% of the water the next time you mix. In grams, that's 10–15 g less water per 500 g of flour.
