## A Pizza Built for October

There is a specific kind of October night that calls for this pizza. Hoodie on, oven heating up, daylight gone by 6pm. You want something with weight to it — not a margherita, not a salad pizza. Something that tastes like fall actually happened.

This is that pizza. Smashed Italian sausage, fennel seed, and a low-moisture mozzarella that gets properly brown at the edges. It is not subtle.

## What "Smash Style" Means Here

We are not slicing sausage or crumbling it loosely. **Smash style** means you press raw Italian sausage — out of the casing or bulk — directly onto the raw pizza skin before it goes into the oven. Flat, thin pieces, pressed down with your palm or fingers.

It cooks faster than a thick crumble. The edges of each piece crisp up and char slightly. The fat renders into the sauce underneath. You get texture in every bite.

Loose crumbles tend to steam in a hot oven. Smashed pieces sear.

## The Build

**What you need for one 12-inch pizza:**

- Dough ball, room temp, ready to open
- 3 to 4 ounces bulk Italian sausage — hot or sweet, your call
- 3 tablespoons crushed tomato or no-cook tomato sauce — thin enough to spread without weighing the dough down
- 3 ounces low-moisture mozzarella, torn or sliced
- 1 teaspoon fennel seed, lightly crushed
- Red pepper flakes, a pinch of flaky salt, a drizzle of olive oil after the bake

**To build it:**

Open your dough by hand and get it onto a lightly semolina-dusted peel. On the Ninja, brush it with oil and par-bake it for about 1 minute 30 seconds before building. Spread sauce thin — edge to edge, just barely. Lay the mozzarella down. Then pinch off small pieces of sausage and press them flat directly onto the cheese, spaced so they can actually crisp rather than steam each other out. Hit the whole thing with the crushed fennel seed before it goes in.

## Cooking It

We cook this the way we cook every pizza on our Ninja Woodfire: preheat to 550°F (about 20 minutes, with the stone reading around 540°F on an infrared thermometer), brush the stretched dough with oil and par-bake it for about 1 minute 30 seconds, then top it and bake for about 5 minutes without turning it. Thin, smashed sausage has time to cook through and brown at 550°F; thick crumbles would not, which is the whole reason for pressing it flat.

In a hotter Neapolitan-style oven (800°F and up), expect about 90 seconds with frequent turning, and watch that the meat doesn't char before the dough is done.

Watch the edges of the smashed meat. When they go dark brown and slightly crisp, you are there.

## After the Bake

Pull it. Hit it with flaky salt and a small drizzle of good olive oil while it is still hot. The fennel seed blooms in the residual heat. Red pepper flakes if you want the heat.

Let it rest for 90 seconds before you cut it. Sausage fat needs a moment to settle.
