Hardwood pellets are compressed sawdust, dried to a low moisture content so they burn cleanly. When they soak up moisture from the air, they swell, crumble, and burn poorly.

On a Ninja Woodfire, that matters in a specific way. The oven heats with electricity — the pellets are only there for smoke. Damp pellets won't stop your pizza from cooking, but they'll smolder instead of burning cleanly, and you lose the wood-fired flavor you added them for. They can also leave dust and crumbs in the hopper.

Coastal New England summers are humid, so it's worth a little attention.

## Tells that pellets have gone soft

- The pellets crumble into sawdust when you pinch one.
- There's more dust than pellets at the bottom of the bag or the hopper.
- The smoke looks gray and heavy instead of thin and pale, or it smells acrid.

If a bag has gotten away from you, don't fight it. Pellet dust composts fine, and a fresh bag will smoke cleaner.

## Simple ways to keep them dry

- **Close the bag between cooks.** An open bag sitting outside on a humid night is the fastest way to ruin pellets.
- **Keep them off the ground.** Pellets stored on a deck or a garage floor pick up moisture faster than pellets on a shelf.
- **Avoid hot, damp spots.** A sealed garage shelf beats a shed in August.
- **Only load what you'll use.** The Ninja's hopper takes about half a cup for roughly an hour of smoke. Leftover pellets sitting in the hopper for days are exposed to the weather.

Always use food-grade pellets made for grills and smokers. Heating pellets can contain binders that aren't meant for food.
