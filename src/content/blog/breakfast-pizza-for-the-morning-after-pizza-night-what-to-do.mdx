You made too much dough last night. It happens to us constantly. One ball sits in the fridge wrapped in plastic, slightly deflated, and you are not sure if it is still worth using.

It is. Make breakfast pizza.

## What Leftover Dough Actually Does Overnight

Cold fermentation keeps working even after you planned to stop it. A dough ball that sat overnight in the fridge will be more extensible than it was fresh — easier to stretch, a little more complex in flavor. It is not overproofed if you used it within 24 hours or so. You are just working with a dough that has had time to relax.

Pull it from the fridge about 45 minutes before you cook. Let it come up toward room temperature on a lightly floured board.

## The Breakfast Pizza We Keep Coming Back To

No tomato sauce. This one runs on a thin layer of whole-milk ricotta — just enough to coat — with a few eggs cracked directly onto the pie before it goes in the oven. Finish with torn prosciutto, a handful of chives, and a pinch of red pepper flake.

Here is how we build it:

- **Ricotta base** — spread it thin, maybe 3 tablespoons for a 12-inch pie, seasoned with salt and a little black pepper
- **Eggs** — crack 2 to 3 directly on top before the launch; they will set in the oven
- **Prosciutto** — add it after the cook, not before, so it does not go leathery
- **Chives and flake** — right at the end, still hot off the stone

If you have a soft cheese sitting around — fontina works, fresh mozzarella works — tear a little of that on before the eggs go in.

## Cooking It the Morning After

We usually fire up the Ooni Koda 12 for dinner sessions, but on a Saturday morning with coffee in hand, the Solo Stove Pi is more forgiving. You are not trying to hit 950°F at 8am. A cook at 700 to 750°F with the flame turned low gives the eggs enough time to set without blowing out the edges.

If you are cooking indoors, a cast iron pan or sheet pan in a 500°F oven works fine. Preheat the pan in the oven first, then build directly on it.

The whole cook takes under 10 minutes. That is the entire point.

## Honest Assessment

The egg placement matters more than you think. Crack them toward the center — they will spread. If you crack them near the edge, you will lose one off the side during the launch. We learned this after two or three breakfast sessions.

Also: the dough may be a little stiffer than you expect right out of the fridge. Do not force it. Let it rest longer if it keeps springing back.

Breakfast pizza is not a compromise for leftover dough. It is a reason to make more dough than you need.

## Try This Next

If you want the eggs to set more evenly, tent a piece of foil loosely over the pie for the last 60 seconds of the cook. It traps heat on top without burning the crust underneath.
