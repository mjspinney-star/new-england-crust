Some pizzas you make once to say you made them. This is not that pizza. Hot honey pepperoni is the one we come back to every single week — August cookouts, October sessions with a hoodie on, the random Tuesday when nobody feels like deciding what's for dinner.

It earns that rotation.

## What You Need

This is a short list. That's not a selling point — it's just the truth.

- Stretched pizza dough (cold-fermented if you have it; same-day works fine)
- **Crushed canned tomatoes** — San Marzano or a quality domestic brand, seasoned with salt and a splash of olive oil
- Whole-milk low-moisture mozzarella, torn or sliced
- **Cupped pepperoni** — the kind that curls into little grease pools in the oven, not the flat deli variety
- Hot honey — store-bought is fine, homemade is better
- Dried oregano and a pinch of flake salt for finishing

That's it. Do not add more.

## How We Build It

Sauce goes on first — thin layer, edge to edge. We use a no-cook tomato sauce we keep in the fridge all summer. Do not drown the dough. You want coverage, not soup.

Mozzarella goes on next. Leave some gaps. The sauce showing through is good.

Pepperoni goes on top of the cheese. This is important — cheese under pepperoni means the cups hold their shape and catch the rendered fat. If you put cheese on top, you lose that.

No hot honey yet. It goes on after the cook.

## The Cook

We run this in the Ooni Koda 12 at around 850 to 900°F. Launch it, turn it once at about 45 seconds, pull it at 60 to 75 seconds total. The pepperoni should be charred at the edges and blistered in the centers.

If you're cooking on a Solo Stove Pi or something with a wider temperature window, you're looking at 90 seconds to two minutes. Watch the undercarriage — lift with your turning peel at the 60-second mark and check.

Stone temperature matters here more than anything else. Get your **infrared thermometer** on the stone before every launch.

## Finishing

Pull the pizza. Let it sit for thirty seconds — just long enough so the hot honey doesn't instantly run off every surface.

Drizzle the hot honey across the top. Not a flood. You want a pass over each slice, hitting some of the pepperoni cups directly. The fat pooled in those cups mixes with the honey and does something you will understand the first time you taste it.

Pinch of flake salt. Dried oregano. Cut and serve.

## One Honest Note

The hot honey is easy to overdo. More does not mean better here. A light hand gives you heat and sweetness that backs up the pepperoni. A heavy hand makes everything sticky and sweet and weird. Start with less than you think you need.

You will calibrate this after the first cook.

## Try This Next

If your dough isn't cold-fermented, try building in 72 hours — the flavor underneath a topping this bold is worth developing. And if you're not sure when your stone is actually ready, a quick read of our infrared thermometer post will change how you time your preheat.
