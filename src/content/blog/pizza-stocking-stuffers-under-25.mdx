Finding a gift for someone who already owns an outdoor pizza oven is harder than it sounds. The oven itself is taken care of. What's left tends to be either too cheap to bother with or well over budget. The sweet spot — useful, specific, under $25 — takes some hunting.

Here are the things worth stuffing in a stocking for a backyard pizza cook.

## An Infrared Thermometer

This is the one we'd push hardest. Before we had one, we sometimes pulled pale-bottomed pizzas off a stone that looked ready but wasn't. With an infrared thermometer, you just point and shoot — you know exactly where your stone stands before you commit a dough ball to it. We have a longer look at how to use one over at [How to Read Your Pizza Stone with an Infrared Thermometer](/blog/2026-06-16-reading-your-stone-with-an-infrared-thermometer/). Most decent models land well under $25 and make a real difference in consistency.

## Semolina Flour

A one- or two-pound bag of semolina is an underrated gift. It slides dough off a peel more reliably than all-purpose flour — coarser, less sticky, less likely to absorb into the dough and gum things up mid-launch. If the person on your list is still fighting stuck dough, this is a cheap fix. We wrote about exactly why at [The Case for Semolina on the Peel](/blog/2026-07-09-the-case-for-semolina-on-the-peel-what-it-does-tha/).

## A Bench Scraper

Metal, rigid, with a flat edge. It's how you divide dough cleanly, how you move a sticky ball without tearing it, and how you scrape down a floured board in one pass. It costs next to nothing and most pizza cooks either don't own one or are using a flimsy plastic version. Get the metal one.

## Good Finishing Olive Oil

A small bottle of a decent finishing oil — not cooking oil, something with actual flavor — costs under $25 and goes a long way. A drizzle after the bake makes a real difference on a white pie or anything with fresh mozzarella. We talked through when it actually helps at [Finishing with Good Olive Oil: When It Matters and When It Does Not](/blog/2026-06-22-finishing-with-good-olive-oil-when-it-matters-and-/).

## A Proofing Tray with a Lid

If they're still using mismatched bowls covered in plastic wrap to cold-ferment dough balls, a dedicated proofing tray is a genuine upgrade. The stackable kind — flat-bottomed, lidded — keeps dough balls properly separated and covered in the fridge for 24–48 hours without drying out the surface. Many fit in a standard fridge shelf. Most are well under $25.

## Hot Honey

A small bottle of hot honey is an easy, low-risk stocking stuffer for anyone who makes pizza. It works as a finisher on sausage pies, on anything with a salty cheese, and on plain tomato pies where the sweetness has somewhere to go. It also completely overwhelms some combinations — we covered the specifics at [Hot Honey on Pizza: When It Works and When It Overwhelms Everything Else](/blog/2026-07-16-hot-honey-on-pizza-when-it-works-and-when-it-overw/). A small bottle is the right gift because it's something to experiment with, not something to commit a whole pizza to sight unseen.

## Baleine Fine Sea Salt

Sounds boring. Isn't. Salt matters in dough, and a lot of cooks are using the wrong grind or the wrong amount because they grabbed whatever was on the counter. A nice tin of fine sea salt — this is what we use in [our dough recipe](/recipes/caputo-breadmaker-dough/) — is a small, thoughtful detail that most people won't buy for themselves.

## A Pizza Dough Scraper (The Plastic Kind)

Different from the bench scraper above — this one is flexible, curved plastic, used to get dough out of a bowl cleanly. Cheap, useful, easy to lose. Most serious pizza cooks are glad to have a spare.
