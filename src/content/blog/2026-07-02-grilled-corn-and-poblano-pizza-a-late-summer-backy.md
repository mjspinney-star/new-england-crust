August in New England is short. You know this. So when the farm stand has real corn — the kind that smells like something — and the grocery store actually has decent poblanos, we stop what we are doing and make this pizza.

It is not a year-round pie. That is the whole point.

## What You Need

This one is light on ingredients, which means everything has to actually taste like something.

- 2 ears of corn, shucked
- 1 large poblano pepper
- Low-moisture mozzarella, shredded — about 4 ounces per pie
- One ball of dough, room temperature *[See our 72-hour cold ferment dough for best results]*
- Olive oil, salt, black pepper
- Optional: a small handful of crumbled cotija for finishing

Skip the red sauce entirely. This pie uses olive oil as the base, and the char from the corn and poblano does the heavy lifting.

## Grill the Corn and Poblano First

Get your grill hot — or fire up whatever outdoor oven you are running. We use the **Ooni Koda 12** *[Affiliate link: Ooni Koda 12 — Ooni]* for the pizza itself, but a standard gas or charcoal grill handles the corn and poblano step well.

Grill the corn directly over the flame, turning until you get real char marks on most of the kernels. Cut the kernels off the cob once it is cool enough to handle. Char the poblano until the skin blisters all over, then seal it in a bowl with a plate on top for ten minutes. Peel, seed, and slice it into thin strips.

This step takes about twenty minutes. Do not rush it — the char is the flavor.

## Building the Pizza

Stretch your dough to about 11 or 12 inches. Brush the surface with olive oil, then scatter the mozzarella across it, leaving an inch at the edge. Lay the corn kernels and poblano strips over the cheese. Season with salt and black pepper.

Launch it into your oven. At 950F on the Koda, it cooks in about 75 to 90 seconds and needs a turn halfway through — a **turning peel** makes this much easier at high heat. At lower temps on something like the **Solo Stove Pi** *[Affiliate link: Solo Stove Pi — Solo Stove]*, give it 3 to 4 minutes.

Pull it when the crust is spotted and the cheese has color on the edges.

## Honest Assessment

The poblano can vary a lot. Some are mild and almost sweet. Some hit harder than expected. You will not know until you taste one, so sample a strip before building the pie. If it is aggressively hot, use less.

Also: grilled corn loses its magic fast. Build and cook this pizza within an hour of cutting the kernels, or the sweetness fades.

## Finishing

A drizzle of good olive oil right off the oven. Cotija if you have it — the salt and crumble contrast works well here. That is all it needs.

This is a one-season pie. Make it while the corn is actually good, eat it outside before the weather turns, and that is enough.
