Buying a gift for someone who already owns an outdoor pizza oven is harder than it sounds. The oven itself is the obvious purchase — everything after that is a gamble. We've been cooking on the Ninja Woodfire for over a year, and a handful of accessories have earned a permanent place in our setup. The rest have not.

If you want the longer version of our thinking, our [Ninja Woodfire accessories guide](/blog/ninja-woodfire-accessories-worth-buying/) covers the full breakdown. This post is the short list — the things we'd actually want to unwrap.

---

## An Infrared Thermometer

This is the one we'd hand to any new Ninja owner first. Before we had one, we occasionally pulled pizzas with pale, undercooked bottoms — not a consistent problem, but an annoying one. Now we confirm the stone temperature before every bake. At our usual 550°F setting, the stone reads around 540°F when it's ready. At 700°F, it reads around 695°F. That number tells you when to launch.

You can read more about how to use one in our post on [reading your pizza stone with an infrared thermometer](/blog/2026-06-16-reading-your-stone-with-an-infrared-thermometer/). It's one of those tools that seems optional until you use it consistently, and then you can't imagine skipping it.

**Takeaway:** If the person you're buying for doesn't already own one, this is the gift.

---

## A Turning Peel

We own one, and it gets used. A turning peel isn't about spinning the pizza mid-bake — our routine at 550°F doesn't call for that. It helps with launching cleanly, checking the underside without fumbling, and general maneuvering inside the oven. Whether it's essential depends on how much someone is already cooking. Our post [Two Peels vs. One](/blog/2026-06-01-two-peels-vs-one-is-a-turning-peel-worth-owning/) gets into the honest case for and against it.

**Takeaway:** A thoughtful gift for someone who's been cooking for a while and wants a little more control at the oven.

---

## Wood Pellets — A Sampler, Not a Bulk Bag

One thing that surprises people about the Ninja Woodfire: the pellets don't heat the oven. It runs on electricity. The pellets exist entirely for smoke flavor, and they do add something — a subtle, woodsy note that you wouldn't get otherwise. We keep oak, maple, and a cherry blend on hand and switch between them depending on what we're making.

A small assortment of flavors is a genuinely useful gift for a Ninja owner, especially if they've only ever used one type. Our [gear page](/gear/) lists what we use. Just make sure you buy pellets sized for this oven — not all pellets are the same.

**Takeaway:** Low cost, genuinely useful, and something they might not buy for themselves.

---

## A Heavy-Duty Cover

The Ninja Woodfire lives outside on our deck, and coastal New England is not gentle — rain, wind, and humidity are constants for most of the season. We tested a third-party heavy-duty cover through two months of rough fall weather and it held up without issues. It's not a glamorous gift, but it's a practical one for anyone who stores their oven outdoors.

For more on what happens when an outdoor oven goes unprotected, our post [What Actually Happens to an Outdoor Oven Left Uncovered in New England](/blog/cover-vs-no-cover-what-actually-happens-to-an-outdoor-oven-left/) is worth a read before you decide.

**Takeaway:** If they don't already have a cover, this protects a real investment.
