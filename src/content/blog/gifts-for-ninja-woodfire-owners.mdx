Buying for someone who already owns an outdoor pizza oven is harder than it looks. The oven was the big purchase. Everything after it is a guess — and we know from experience how easy it is to guess wrong, because we bought too many accessories in our first few months and a few of them have never left the box.

We've cooked on a Ninja Woodfire for over a year ([here's exactly how we cook pizza on it](/blog/how-we-cook-pizza-on-the-ninja-woodfire/)). This is the short list of things we'd actually want to unwrap, with one quick check for each so you don't buy something they already own. If you want the full owner's breakdown instead, that's our [Ninja Woodfire accessories guide](/blog/ninja-woodfire-accessories-worth-buying/).

## The Best Gift Under $25: An Infrared Thermometer

If we could give a new Ninja owner one thing, it's this. The oven's display shows air temperature, not the temperature of the stone — and the stone is what cooks the bottom of the pizza. Before we owned one, we'd occasionally pull a pie with a pale, underdone base. Since then, we check the stone before every launch and that problem is gone. Our full method is in [reading your pizza stone with an infrared thermometer](/blog/2026-06-16-reading-your-stone-with-an-infrared-thermometer/).

We use the <AffiliateLink id="infrared-thermometer" label="Etekcity infrared thermometer" />. It runs about $20.

**Check first:** a meat thermometer is not the same thing. If they only have a probe or instant-read thermometer, this is still a new tool for them.

## The Gift They Didn't Know They Needed: A Long Outdoor Cord

The Ninja Woodfire ships with a 4.5-foot power cord. Almost every owner discovers this the hard way, usually the first night the oven ends up farther from the outlet than planned. A <AffiliateLink id="extension-cord" label="12-gauge outdoor-rated extension cord" /> fixes it. The 12-gauge rating matters — the oven pulls real wattage, and a thin indoor cord can overheat.

**Check first:** ask (casually) where the oven sits relative to the outlet. If the answer involves a cord they grabbed from the garage, this is the gift.

## A Pellet Sampler, Not a Bulk Bag

Something that surprises people: the pellets don't heat the Ninja. It runs on electricity. The pellets are there purely for smoke flavor, and most owners settle on one bag and never branch out. A variety of woods is a gift they probably wouldn't buy for themselves.

- The <AffiliateLink id="kona-pellets" label="Kona variety pack" /> — eight one-pound bags of different single woods. The best way to find out what they like without committing to a big bag of something they don't.
- <AffiliateLink id="all-purpose-pellets" label="Ninja's All-Purpose Blend" /> (cherry, maple, oak) — the safe pick if you'd rather give one bag. Mild enough for pizza, chicken, and vegetables.

**Check first:** buy **food-grade** pellets only. Heating pellets — the kind sold for pellet stoves — can contain binders that aren't meant to go near food. Both picks above are food-grade.

## A Better Launching Peel (Before a Turning Peel)

We own a small turning peel, and it's nice to have for checking the underside of a pie or nudging it around. But at the 550°F we usually cook at, we don't rotate pizzas, so it's optional — our post [Two Peels vs. One](/blog/2026-06-01-two-peels-vs-one-is-a-turning-peel-worth-owning/) makes the honest case for and against.

The peel that matters is the one you launch with. A perforated aluminum peel lets excess flour fall through instead of burning on the stone. The <AffiliateLink id="ninja-peel" label="Ninja XSKOPPL perforated peel" /> is sized for this oven; a <AffiliateLink id="generic-peel" label="generic 12-inch perforated peel" /> does the same job for less.

**Check first:** if they're launching off a wooden board or a solid metal peel, a perforated launching peel is the upgrade. If they already have one, then a turning peel makes a fine gift for someone who cooks a lot.

## What to Skip

A few gifts that look thoughtful but mostly end up in a drawer:

- **A fancy pizza pan or upgraded stone.** The stone that comes with the oven is the right tool. We explain why in [why a cheap pizza stone still beats an expensive pan](/blog/why-a-cheap-pizza-stone-still-beats-an-expensive-pan/).
- **Extra pellet scoops.** The oven comes with one. Nobody needs a second.
- **Laminated "cooking cheat sheets."** Ninja's own quick-start guide covers the basics, and the rest is one search away.
- **Specialty cleaning kits.** A scraper and a dry brush handle almost all of it, and the dark patina on the stone is supposed to be there.
