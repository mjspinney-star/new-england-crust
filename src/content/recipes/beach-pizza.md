ingredients:
  - "500 g bread flour"
  - "325 g warm water (65% hydration)"
  - "7 g instant yeast"
  - "10 g fine sea salt"
  - "15 g olive oil, plus more for the pan"
  - "1 can (28 oz) crushed tomatoes"
  - "2 tbsp tomato paste"
  - "1 tbsp sugar (do not skip — this is the beach pizza signature)"
  - "1 tsp dried oregano"
  - "1/2 tsp garlic powder"
  - "1/2 tsp salt for the sauce"
  - "8–10 slices deli provolone (whole slices, overlapping — never shredded)"
instructions:
  - step: "Make the dough"
    text: "Mix flour, water, yeast, salt, and olive oil until no dry flour remains. Knead 8 minutes by hand or 5 in a mixer until smooth. Cover and rest 2 hours at room temp until doubled."
  - step: "Make the sweet sauce"
    text: "Stir together crushed tomatoes, tomato paste, sugar, oregano, garlic powder, and salt. No cooking needed — it cooks on the pizza. Taste it: it should read noticeably sweet. That's correct."
  - step: "Pan and stretch"
    text: "Oil a half-sheet pan generously. Press the dough to the edges and corners. If it fights back, rest 10 minutes and press again. It should be thin — about 1/4 inch."
  - step: "Sauce edge to edge"
    text: "Spread a thin, even layer of sauce all the way to the edges. Beach pizza has no bare rim."
  - step: "Lay the provolone"
    text: "Shingle whole provolone slices across the pie, overlapping slightly. Gaps are fine — the slices melt into pools, not a uniform blanket. That's the look."
  - step: "Bake hot"
    text: "Bake at 500°F on the lowest rack for 12–14 minutes, until the bottom is golden and the provolone is melted with browned spots."
  - step: "Cut into squares"
    text: "Rest 3 minutes, then cut into squares. Eat one standing over the pan like you're at the beach. Mandatory."

If you grew up anywhere north of Boston, you know this pizza. Thin, a little sweet, provolone laid on in slices, sold by the square at Tripoli's and Cristy's on the Salisbury Beach boardwalk. It doesn't taste like any other pizza in America, and that's the point.

The two non-negotiables: sugar in the sauce, and whole provolone slices instead of shredded mozzarella. Everything else is forgiving. Here's my home version, tested until my kids couldn't tell the difference.
