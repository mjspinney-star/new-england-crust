Three ingredients. One machine. The rest is time.

This is our everyday dough — the one behind the pizzas on our Ninja. It's light and extensible, with a thin, pliable center and a rim that puffs and browns on the stone. [Here's why we cold-ferment it](/blog/72-hour-cold-ferment-dough/). Caputo 00 is the classic Neapolitan flour, and there's a reason. It's milled finer than anything you'll find at a grocery store, the gluten develops beautifully in a bread machine, and the result stretches without fighting you.

The three ingredients in the photo — <AffiliateLink id="caputo-flour" label="Antimo Caputo 00 Flour" />, <AffiliateLink id="caputo-yeast" label="Caputo Lievito dry yeast" />, and <AffiliateLink id="baleine-sea-salt" label="Baleine fine sea salt" /> — are the whole recipe. Use a kitchen scale. It's not optional with 00 flour.

---

**Hydration: 65%.** This is on the lower end of Neapolitan doughs — intentionally. Bread machines work best with slightly stiffer doughs. You can nudge it to 68% (340g water) once you've made it a few times and know how your machine handles it.

---

## Notes

**On water temperature.** We use cool tap water, not cold from the fridge, not warm. Bread machines generate heat during kneading — warm water can push the dough temperature too high and overactivate the yeast before fermentation starts.

**On yeast.** 1.5g is not a typo. It's about a quarter of what most American recipes call for. Less yeast + more time = better flavor. Trust the cold ferment.

**On the olive oil.** It's optional and traditional Neapolitan dough skips it entirely. We add it when we want a softer crumb. Leave it out when we want maximum char on the rim.

**On yield.** 500 g of flour makes about 840 g of dough. We usually go with three 10-inch pies at about 280 g each — good coverage, easy to manage on the peel. Four balls of about 210 g each make smaller pies.

Making a different number of balls? The [dough calculator](/tools/dough-calculator/) scales this recipe and tells you when to mix.

**On the machine.** We mix ours in a <AffiliateLink id="cuisinart-bread-maker" label="Cuisinart CBK-110" /> on the Dough cycle, which handles the kneading and first rise. Pull the dough out, ball it, and cold-ferment 24–48 hours.
